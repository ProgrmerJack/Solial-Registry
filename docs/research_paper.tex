\section{Legal mandate, responsible developers and purpose}
This supporting analytical material accompanies the citizen proposal by Abduxoliq Akmal ugli Ashuraliyev. It is prepared for the National Agency for Social Protection under the President of the Republic of Uzbekistan and the Ministry of Economy and Finance of the Republic of Uzbekistan to consider in the mandated draft. The author is not presenting an explanatory note on behalf of those official developers. The purpose is to justify the proposed seven operative provisions and twenty-eight-clause verification annex; the primary Russian explanatory note and its Uzbek and English companion translations organize the submission information under paragraphs 83–85 of the unified drafting methodology. \cite{ref7}.

The immediate legislative opportunity is specific. Paragraphs 13–14 of PF-193, dated 11 September 2026, introduce multidimensional assessment from 1 January 2027 and direct the National Agency for Social Protection and Ministry of Economy and Finance to submit the implementing draft to the Presidential Administration by 1 December 2026. Paragraph 14 does not identify the final instrument’s legal form. Accordingly, this paper proposes an insertion module and consequential amendments for the competent developer to integrate, rather than claim that a completed Cabinet resolution alone fulfils the mandate. \cite{ref1}.

Household classification has at least three distinct meanings: a calculation produces a score or income measure; a legally specified process assigns a registry category; and a particular programme determines entitlement and payment. Combining these stages into a single opaque output makes it difficult to identify whether a disputed result arose from source data, arithmetic, an exception, a category rule or a separate programme condition. A reproducible system must preserve those distinctions even where the same information system executes them.

Existing law supplies an operational baseline. Cabinet Resolution 35 of 29 January 2026 establishes registry rules, income calculations, reassessment, information exchange and appeal arrangements. The consolidated text retrieved on the working date includes amendments made during 2026. PF-258 of 26 December 2025 supplies higher-ranking category provisions and hardship conditions. These instruments remain material to implementation unless the new act lawfully amends them. A new scoring model does not, by itself, repeal existing refusal grounds or grant a programmer authority to reconcile inconsistent legal text. \cite{ref2}; \cite{ref3}.

PF-193 paragraph 13 confirms financial circumstances, chronic illness, disability, external labour migration and multiple children as assessment dimensions, with scoring-based category assignment from 1 January 2027. Paragraphs 13–14 of that decree do not specify numerical weights, transformations, rounding or category thresholds; they mandate development of the implementing act. The primary decree, the Agency consultation listing and public indexed searches were checked on 1 October 2026 without obtaining a complete 2027 numerical specification. This records the scope and result of retrieval, not proof that no internal or public specification exists. The Article 22 request asks for the dated specification and its approval or draft status. \cite{ref1}.

The research question is therefore deliberately falsifiable: \textbf{do the selected published rules produce reproducible, internally coherent income and category predicates across their stated boundaries and declared input domains?} A transcription error, an applicable omitted exception, a legally narrower domain or disagreement with independent arithmetic would defeat a corresponding finding. The question does not ask whether a newly fitted model predicts poverty well. No 2027 weights or Agency household records were supplied, so that second question cannot be answered here.

The proposed participation route is Article 22 of O‘RQ-682. It permits consenting citizens with relevant knowledge and experience to participate in drafting groups; participation is discretionary and qualification must be evidenced. Article 20 supports submission of proposals. Article 24 governs consultation and justification of rejected proposals. \cite{ref7}.

\section{Existing regulatory practice and relevant evidence}
\subsection{Retrieval and currency}
This is a targeted narrative review of primary documents, not a systematic review. Official consolidated legislation, official statistics, publisher pages, author-hosted published research and World Bank reports were retrieved during the project. Relevant provisions, abstracts and selected full-text sections were inspected. Sources that could not be retrieved beyond a search snippet were excluded from the substantive synthesis. Direct retrieval was used because the research-lookup provider was not configured. Searches for later corrections and current official instruments were checks on currency; they do not prove that no correction, unpublished draft or unindexed amendment exists.

Legal texts are cited by instrument and provision. For large reports, printed pages are distinguished from PDF page numbers. The 2026 Uzbekistan assessment’s printed page 40 is PDF page 42; the delivery-systems sourcebook’s printed page 141 is PDF page 171. The source selection is sufficient to motivate the proposed verification architecture, but it cannot establish that this architecture is uniquely optimal. Foreign evidence is used to identify questions and mechanisms, not to import coefficients or expected Uzbek effects.

\subsection{Contemporary Uzbekistan evidence}
Official SIAT statistics report national poverty rates of 17.0, 14.1, 11.0, 8.9 and 5.8 percent for 2021–2025. These published aggregates provide context; they are not observations used to calibrate this audit or evidence that a particular registry algorithm caused the decline. Regional poverty rates cannot validate individual household classifications. \cite{ref6}.

The World Bank’s Uzbekistan poverty and equity assessment, published on 18 September 2026, discusses coverage and targeting of low-income family assistance. Its printed page 40 notes that effectiveness evidence for the formula revised in early 2026 was not yet available. Its technical annex describes the income-based statistical welfare aggregate. That aggregate should not be treated as identical to the administrative measure, which includes legally imputed income and specific inclusions and exclusions. The report supports an empirical research need; it does not validate the 2027 scoring specification not obtained for this project or demonstrate operational incidence of this paper’s counterexamples. \cite{ref11}.

\subsection{Observed service evidence and what it can establish}
The Government's report of 17 July 2026 records 2\,000 unjustified refusals in 184 mahallas in one month, and registry-related Presidential appeals rising from 30\,000 to 46\,000. It directs prosecutorial oversight. This documents a reason to examine decision grounds; it supplies no software-cause attribution, national denominator, present incidence or estimate of the proposed control's effect. The cause cannot be inferred from a formula counterexample. \cite{ref28}.

The official service 587 page, observed on 1 October 2026, describes an application/result channel, free service, no required documents and a stated one-month period. It displays 640\,876 total statuses including 16\,564 in process. No accumulation window, pending-age distribution, handling times, extraction date or staff availability is given. These counters are not annual demand, a count of overdue decisions or capacity. A February 19 Urganch report documents local practical training on Resolution No. 35, lawful inclusion/removal and data reliability; it does not prove national staffing readiness. Existing channels and training processes can be reused, but their incremental workload needs actual operational records. \cite{ref29}; \cite{ref30}.

Agency-specific release records, process timings, contract coverage and funded assignments were not obtained. Public financial-transparency pages and broader social-protection project documents did not establish an allocation or spare capacity for this module. The proposal therefore makes no queue forecast, staffing estimate or delivery-time guarantee. If integration changes application handling, the Agency must use dated arrivals/completions, pending ages, source delays and processing times in an authorized capacity check before committing that change; aggregate budgets or posts cannot substitute for those observations.

\subsection{What international evidence contributes}
The 2025 World Bank review of social registries discusses dynamic updating, on-demand access, interoperability and administrative capacity. Its discussion supports treating a registry as part of a delivery process: database coverage and programme eligibility are different objects. It supplies no transferable Uzbek acceptance threshold. \cite{ref12}.

The Indonesia data-quality assessment emphasizes common standards, freshness, audit and local implementation capacity. These considerations motivate a variable passport and explicit correction pathway here. The mechanism that could transfer is improved traceability of source values; the size of any benefit depends on Uzbek source systems and staffing, which were not observed. \cite{ref13}.

The delivery-systems sourcebook separates assessment from eligibility, enrolment, notification and benefit provision. Its grievance discussion includes errors arising at different delivery stages. This distinction supports retaining calculation records and routing a correction to the responsible process rather than equating a score with payment. Historical country examples in the book are not presented as current institutional facts. \cite{ref14}.

The 2022 targeting synthesis places policy objectives, programme design, targeting choice and implementation in a hierarchy. It also cautions that the payoff from more complex models depends on data and context. That supports first specifying what “correct” means before optimizing a score. \cite{ref15}.

Two primary studies illustrate why welfare validation needs a declared comparator. Alatas and colleagues’ randomized Indonesian study compared proxy-means, community and hybrid targeting in 640 villages: ranking against consumption and participant satisfaction did not identify an identical preferred method. This is evidence from one setting, not an estimate for Uzbekistan. Aiken and colleagues’ Togo study found that performance of phone-data targeting depended on whether the comparison was geographical targeting or a hypothetical comprehensive registry. Neither study supports treating an old administrative decision as an unquestionable welfare label. \cite{ref16}; \cite{ref17}.

Together these sources support a separation of \textbf{rule conformance}, \textbf{data quality}, \textbf{welfare validity} and \textbf{delivery outcomes}. The completed analysis establishes selected conformance properties. The other dimensions remain a proposed Agency research programme.

\section{Inputs and method of the supporting calculation}
\subsection{Data objects and units}
The inputs are published legal constants and constructed cases. The 2026 monthly minimum consumption expenditure per person is denoted P = 715,000 soum. Monthly minimum remuneration is M = 1,360,000 soum from 1 September 2026. The former is taken from the National Statistics Committee’s 4 March 2026 announcement; the latter from PF-115. M is used only for dated September single-month diagnostics. It is not substituted retrospectively into every month of a three-month income history. \cite{ref5}; \cite{ref4}.

Let Y be included household income over the selected three months and N be the legally relevant number of members. The monthly per-person measure is $D=Y/(3N)$. This paper does not reconstruct the complete Y aggregation from administrative data. It audits selected contribution formulas and category predicates. Contributions described as monthly must be converted to period totals once, before division by three; a second division or omission of the period conversion changes the meaning of D.

The reference implementation is \nolinkurl{scripts/run_analysis.py}. Numeric inputs are integers or rational/decimal strings and calculations use Python’s exact Fraction type. Floating-point inputs, invalid divisors, malformed values, duplicate fields and unsupported configuration fields are rejected. Exact arithmetic prevents an accidental binary approximation from deciding an equality test. Production rounding still requires an authoritative rule: this study does not invent a rounding convention and attribute it to the Agency.

\subsection{Prespecification and selection}
The original audit was specified in the supplied prespecified analysis plan before execution, following exploratory reading of the provisions. Its candidates were therefore known in advance; it is a formal diagnostic, not a blinded confirmatory population study. The later extension retained the approved method and froze its cases before execution. A counterexample arising from the first independent review was explicitly labelled post-hoc and retained as a regression check.

The original income grid uses P, 1.5P and 2P with offsets of minus one, zero and plus one soum, plus a former-category control at P. The extension examines hardship values around P, 4P/3, 1.5P and 2P. It enumerates every integer building area B from zero through total plot area U for U equal to 100, 1,000, 1,001 and 2,000 square metres. It examines missing remittances and values around 2M for both country groups under two explicitly conditional time-unit readings. Tax-floor cases use the algebraic crossover plus the same one-soum offsets in all eight regional/sex cells. Informal-income calculations cover those cells, and livestock cases contain 0, 1, 14, 15, 20 and 100 goats.

These grids have no sampling weights or population frame. Counting their failures is a software/domain diagnostic. A confidence interval for the prevalence of affected Uzbek households would be meaningless because no such households were sampled. The grid is exhaustive only within its declared constructed domain, not across every legally or operationally possible state.

\subsection{Legal-computational predicates}
For a new able-to-work applicant without other refusal grounds, former-category status or hardship recommendation, paragraph 33(a) refuses primary recognition on income when D > P. Paragraph 32 recognizes primary status in the absence of paragraph 33 grounds. Paragraph 39(b), however, describes primary-category income as D < P. The audit evaluates these predicates separately; it does not pretend to encode the complete application decision.

For a hardship applicant, define $A=\frac{3}{4}D$. Subject to qualifying circumstances, a valid recommendation, available quota, reassessment and absence of other applicable grounds, paragraphs 34–35 give the income path D > P and A \ensuremath{\leq} 1.5P. The general paragraph 39 wording gives the interval from P to 1.5P. Because its relationship to hardship adjustment is not explicit in that wording, the audit checks both an ordinary-income reading and an adjusted-income reading. A legally applied special-rule priority may resolve the issue. The calculation identifies what that priority must protect.

For land, the literal derived area is $L=U-B-\min\{U/5,200\}$, using square metres. The declared algebraic domain is 0 \ensuremath{\leq} B \ensuremath{\leq} U. Monthly normative land income is L \ensuremath{\times} 0.035M \ensuremath{\times} k / 100, where 100 square metres is one sotix and k is the applicable local tax coefficient. The diagnostic assumes k = 1 openly because actual mahalla coefficients were unavailable. The proposed floor max(0,L) is calculated separately from the literal rule.

For remittances, paragraph 26 applies imputed monthly income cM where transfers are unknown or below 2M; c is three for the listed-country group and two for other countries. The comparison period was not supplied. Conditional model A treats the reported comparison quantity q as monthly. Conditional model B treats it as a three-month total and converts accepted actual transfers to q/3 per month. Both apply the stated imputed monthly contribution. These models expose the need to settle the time unit; neither is claimed to be the operational Agency algorithm.

For entrepreneur income, paragraph 24 gives $E=\max\{5T/6,B_0\}$, where T is the tax total over three months and B\textsubscript{0} is the paragraph 23 monthly base. Paragraph 25 gives 1.05B\textsubscript{0} for persons satisfying its informal-work conditions. Under paragraph 28, a goat contributes 0.07 equivalent cattle heads; the first equivalent head is exempt. The goat-only expression is $G=\max\{0.07g-1,0\}M/2$. Other animal coefficients from Resolution 689 were not transcribed or executed here.

\subsection{Verification strategy}
Verification combines the official published example, hand-derived inequalities, independent integer arithmetic and executable regression tests. The separate \nolinkurl{scripts/verify_results.py} imports no rule engine; it checks all 4,105 land cases, twelve hardship cases, twenty-four tax cases, sixteen conditional remittance cases and six goat cases using equivalent piecewise or integer expressions. The completed scientific regression suite contains 27 tests; ten provenance and independent-check regression tests reject stale, modified or incomplete output. Together, the suite contains 37 tests, covering equality, both sides of thresholds, missing versus zero, negative-weight intervals, invalid input, legal formulas, grid counts and conditional interpretations. A prior fresh-context review found two defects in the initial amendment proposal: a blanket marginal lower bound would exclude a valid hardship case, and a decree-annex provision had been omitted. Both were corrected and the counterexample is retained.

The computational materials have undergone independent workspace review and executable verification. The current suite contains 37 regression tests; the separate arithmetic checker verifies the frozen case inventory. The review supports the stated computational results within their scope. It does not establish that the Agency’s production system implements the same rules or that the official 2027 model not obtained for this project has been validated.

\section{Results of the executed rule audit}
\subsection{Official example and equality}
Paragraph 30’s published example yields 6,480,000/(5 \ensuremath{\times} 3) = \textbf{432,000 soum per person per month}. The difference from the published answer is zero. This establishes the arithmetic convention of that example. The example specifies May–July income for a September 2025 application; the precise reference-month lag should be retained in a formal specification rather than guessed from a general phrase.

At D = P = 715,000, the statement D > P is false and D < P is also false. Under the constructed applicant conditions, primary recognition under paragraph 32 is not refused by the income predicate, but paragraph 39(b) does not provide the corresponding category. The mismatch occurs at equality, not throughout a neighbourhood. The former-primary control at P satisfies the ordinary marginal-income interval, demonstrating why prior status cannot be omitted from a complete decision specification.

PF-258 paragraph 3 also uses a strict below-P condition for primary categories. Consequently, a Cabinet-only change of “below” to “not exceeding” would not fully reconcile the hierarchy. The proposed inclusive-equality option requires coordinated changes to PF-258 paragraph 3(a)–(b), its Annex 1 note (a), and Resolution 35 paragraph 39(a)–(b). It also requires revising the ordinary marginal branch while preserving the special hardship branch. The audit does not establish which equality policy government must choose; it establishes that the adopted choice must be explicit and coherent.

\subsection{Hardship intervals: proof and counterexample}
The special-route income conditions simplify exactly:

\begin{equation}
 D>P,\quad\frac34D\leq\frac32P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P<D\leq2P.
\end{equation}
If the general interval uses ordinary income, its intersection with that route is P < D \ensuremath{\leq} 1.5P. It excludes the special-route interval \textbf{1.5P < D \ensuremath{\leq} 2P}. If the general interval instead uses adjusted income, its lower bound A \ensuremath{\geq} P is equivalent to D \ensuremath{\geq} 4P/3. That reading excludes the special-route interval \textbf{P < D < 4P/3}. Therefore, neither simple substitution of D nor of A into a common interval reproduces the entire special route. An explicit exception or branch precedence is necessary in a machine-executable description. This is an interpretation problem to resolve with the developer, not proof that actual officials are refusing those households.

For P = 715,000, the relevant landmarks are 4P/3 = 2,860,000/3, 1.5P = 1,072,500 and 2P = 1,430,000 soum per person per month. The implementation evaluates twelve constructed values around these boundaries. Their rational representation is retained even when a display rounds a plotted coordinate.

The post-hoc reviewer counterexample has three members and three-month income of 8,580,000 soum. Its ordinary D is 2,860,000/3 and adjusted A is exactly 715,000. Assuming all statutory hardship conditions hold, paragraph 35(b)’s upper bound is satisfied. The abandoned proposal requiring adjusted income strictly above P would exclude it. The corrected draft separates the former-primary ordinary-income branch from the hardship branch and adds no invented lower bound to adjusted hardship income.

\subsection{Land-domain audit}
For U \ensuremath{\leq} 1,000, the literal formula is L = 4U/5 − B, so L < 0 precisely when B > 4U/5. For U > 1,000 it is L = U − B − 200, so L < 0 precisely when B > U − 200. These inequalities provide a separate check of the enumeration. Within 0 \ensuremath{\leq} B \ensuremath{\leq} U, L is bounded below by −min(U/5,200). A physical area cannot be negative, although the existence of operational cadastral restrictions could remove these constructed inputs from admissible records.

\subsection{Remittance units and conditional discontinuities}
The equality q = 2M does not satisfy the below-threshold imputation condition. Under the monthly reading, the listed-country contribution changes from 3M immediately below the comparison threshold to 2M at it: a monthly drop of M = 1,360,000 soum. The other-country reading changes from 2M to 2M and is continuous at that boundary. Under the three-month-total reading, accepted actual income at equality is 2M/3 per month, generating different drops. The sixteen constructed cases preserve both readings, both groups and the missing-value branch.

\subsection{Coefficients, tax floor and fractional livestock}
Paragraph 23 contains eight monthly self-employment coefficients across four geographic classes and two sex categories. \subsection{The methodology passport}
Every consequential variable should have an approved passport identifying its legal purpose, authoritative source, household/person linkage, unit, reference period, valid range, conversion, missing/conflicting/stale treatment and effective-dated parameter. The passport must distinguish a measured value from a normative imputation. A “last updated” timestamp alone is insufficient: an old record may describe the correct reference period, while a newly received record may describe the wrong period.

The category specification should be expressed as a decision table or equivalent formal rules. Each row records applicability, priority, conditions, output and legal authority. The hardship row must retain qualifying circumstances, recommendation, quota and reassessment; an income formula alone cannot grant entry. Separate outputs should record primary recognition, registry category, programme eligibility and amount. An unresolved case is a status requiring lawful resolution, not an invented fourth substantive welfare category.

The proposal requires recording the model and software version, source snapshots, reference periods, applied exceptions and calculation steps. The public description should be available except for lawfully protected information. Restricted material must still be accessible to authorized verification and review personnel. This balances reproducibility with confidentiality; it does not establish a right for the external author to receive microdata.

\subsection{Draftable operative requirements}
The author proposes that the Agency prepare the approved specification with the Ministry, perform verification before first use and material changes, and retain a protocol. A material change is one capable of altering the calculation or category, including a source mapping or temporal conversion; relabelling a field may be immaterial if its semantics remain identical. Proposed annex paragraph 25 assigns technical acceptance to the Agency head or a lawfully authorized deputy, supported by methodology, information-system and legal-service conclusions. This is a proposed allocation of responsibility, not evidence of an existing appointment or power to enact scoring criteria.

Paragraph 26 makes acceptance testable. Every prespecified boundary and exception case needs a legally determined expected result; comparable calculations for the same approved version and identical input values must have no score or category discrepancy after the approved rounding rule; and the data-quality, explanation-access and objection-routing functions must work. Failure requires correction and repetition of affected tests. This is a conformance criterion, not a population accuracy target. It is inappropriate to import an arbitrary 95-percent welfare accuracy requirement into the act when no welfare comparator or population sample exists. The requirement does not suspend lawful application processing or alter the mandated implementation date.

The annex requires exact equality tests, exceptions, incomplete data and physically invalid derived values. It prohibits hiding unresolved cases in a successful denominator or treating synthetic checks as welfare validation. It also requires investigation of affected calculations when a software implementation violates the approved rule. The correction of an individual case, continuation of payments and review deadlines remain governed by applicable law; the proposal supplies no automatic suspension power.

Resolution 35 paragraph 48 already permits temporary algorithm changes during disrupted information exchange. The proposal preserves that power while requiring a recorded legal basis, scope, start time, temporary rule and restoration condition. Calculations made under the temporary version must be identifiable for later lawful verification. This creates evidence for audit without inventing a parallel emergency institution. Paragraph 49’s existing oversight responsibilities likewise remain the starting point.

The proposed explanation requirement records why a decision followed, including imputation and exception logic. A notification SMS alone does not reproduce a calculation. Articles 53–54 of the administrative-procedure law address the content and factual/legal reasons of individual administrative acts; its scope and special procedures still require checking for the relevant decision. Proposed paragraph 19 supplies an operational channel: calculation reasons with the decision through the personal electronic account, with access through an Inson centre for a person who does not use the account. This is an express proposed duty, not a finding that the current interface already offers it. \cite{ref10}.

\subsection{Acceptance evidence, independent answers and coverage}
A successful test requires an independently justified expected answer, not just two matching outputs. Barr et al. describe this test-oracle problem and distinguish specified answers from comparisons with another implementation. NIST IR 8397 distinguishes tests derived from requirements, tests derived from code structure and regression cases retained from earlier defects. These sources support the engineering design of verification; neither is a binding Uzbek eligibility standard. Their guidance does not establish that this project's fixtures exhaust the Agency's system. \cite{ref21}; \cite{ref22}.

For this proposal, the methodology unit and legal service should establish the legal derivation of the expected result; a verifier other than the person responsible for the tested implementation should check it. The record should identify the source provision and version, branch preconditions, input units and periods, intermediate contributions, rounding stage and final outcome. For example, at $D=P$, copying an output from a program that implements $\leq P$ would conceal disagreement with the published strict $<P$ category predicate. Conversely, inserting a universal lower bound into the hardship route would exclude the retained $0.75D=P$ counterexample. These are different questions: whether the code follows the rule, and whether the rule needs amendment. An unresolved interpretation must be referred for a lawful determination; neither program agreement nor a majority vote resolves it.

Annex paragraphs 12 and 26 now require a rule-to-case inventory rather than allowing the selected tests themselves to define completeness. Every applicable boundary, exception, precedence path and reachable interaction must have an expected result; an inapplicable path needs a justified exclusion. Before execution, freeze case identifiers and input values. Afterwards reconcile the inventory with the executed cases, including missing and duplicated identifiers. A passing result on an incomplete list is not completion of the declared test scope. Code coverage can provide additional implementation evidence, but a percentage of executed statements cannot establish coverage of omitted legal rules.

\subsection{Review competence: a demonstrated limit of the existing council route}
Resolution 35, Annex 4 paragraph 26, limits the appeal council's subject matter to questionnaires, conclusions and records prepared by the mahalla seven during registry applications. Paragraph 31 permits rejection of a complaint outside that subject matter. Accordingly, a purely computational dispute cannot simply be labelled a council appeal and assumed to fall within existing competence. The problem is the documented scope of this particular route; it does not demonstrate that all other administrative or judicial remedies are absent. Council review has its own fifteen-day examination period under paragraph 33. \cite{ref2}.

The personal-data law supplies a distinct route. Article 24 permits solely automated consequential decisions under its stated exceptions and requires the owner or operator to explain the procedure and consequences, allow an objection and notify the subject in writing of its examination result within ten days. An objection to such a decision is different from correcting an originating record or challenging a mahalla document. Article 11 separately requires correction or supplementation on the subject’s request within three days and immediate correction of identified inaccurate data; that clock must not be replaced with the ten-day automated-decision objection clock. Proposed paragraph 20 therefore routes the automated-decision objection to the data owner or operator, source correction to the authoritative source, and council complaints within the council's lawful subject matter. It expressly preserves other lawful review routes and creates no additional mandatory pre-court stage. This narrower integration avoids the need to pretend that a generic software clause expands council jurisdiction. \cite{ref9}.

The developer must reconcile complaint intake forms and forwarding rules with these distinct routes. The public notice should identify the competent recipient, subject matter and applicable deadline for each route. A complaint touching several issues should preserve the original receipt record and separately route each issue; forwarding must not reset a statutory deadline. These are proposed workflow obligations to make the existing guarantees usable, not a new general appeal entitlement invented by the researcher.

\subsection{Transition, correction and lawful retention}
Three operations must remain distinct: a new policy changes the approved rule prospectively; a software correction repairs implementation of the rule applicable to a past decision; and a source correction changes evidence about a particular period. Proposed paragraph 27 prohibits using a later-period parameter or new criterion automatically in a historical replay. A recalculation by itself is no independent recovery ground. Any recovery or adverse amendment of an individual decision still requires the relevant substantive and procedural legal basis. Articles 59–61 of the administrative-procedure law make it unsafe to promise either unconditional recovery or unconditional immunity from recovery through a technical annex. \cite{ref10}.

Preserving the original calculation is necessary for a review, but it does not justify indefinite storage of all personal records. Proposed paragraph 28 requires a category-specific lawful retention basis and period, authorized access, correction history and lawful destruction or anonymisation when applicable. Article 17 of the personal-data law and relevant archival duties must be applied together. The exact schedule requires the Agency's records inventory; this paper supplies no invented statutory number of retention years. Public outputs also require cross-table disclosure review because individually innocuous aggregates may identify a small household group when linked. \cite{ref9}.

The proposal contributes to the draft already mandated by PF-193 paragraph 14; the mandate does not oblige the developer to incorporate these particular clauses. Each task should therefore be mapped to an existing power or to an additional duty requiring the competent legal instrument. Internal verification instructions may use existing powers; higher-ranking criteria cannot be changed by a software setting or a lower-ranking act. The verification module and optional policy amendments remain separately decidable. \cite{ref1}; \cite{ref7}.

Article 23 requires the developer to study relevant research and citizens’ submissions and to generalize and use proposals and scientific recommendations. This supplies a consideration route even if working-group appointment is declined. For an actual public or specialist discussion, participants must be acquainted with the draft in advance. Discussion proposals remain advisory and must be considered; Article 24 expressly allows rejection with reasons. Reproducible calculations strengthen the basis for consideration, but neither zero software discrepancies nor a favourable review proves a legal duty to adopt the author’s wording. The submission should request incorporation of the clauses and consideration of the author for the computational-verification drafting group. An accurate CV and evidence of performed work support the Article 22 request. \subsection{Prerequisites and data design}
The following is a protocol for subsequent authorized work, not a completed household analysis. Before extraction, the developer and Agency must freeze the model specification, run period, cohorts, comparison questions, source fields and release rules. The legal basis for processing, necessary data volume, health-information safeguards and research anonymisation must be documented. Replacing identifiers alone does not establish anonymisation. Agency-controlled execution is a protection measure, not an independent legal basis. \cite{ref9}.

Supplied code requires separate operator authorization after checking its contents, version, access rights and outbound connections; protected inputs must not reach external services or public logs. These are proposed safe-execution conditions under paragraph 22, not a claim that the Agency environment is already configured. Applicable storage and processing requirements are checked under current Article \(27^{1}\) of the personal-data law, as amended by O‘RQ-1125 of 26 March 2026. Internal execution alone authorizes neither processing nor disclosure. \cite{ref9}.

The minimal logical dataset comprises an internal case key, application and decision dates, household-size basis, model version, source values and periods, extraction timestamps, data-quality status, applied imputation/exception, reference calculation, recorded category and reason code. Where authorized, corrections, reviews and changed decisions should be linkable without losing the original record. Sensitive variables should be included only where necessary for a legally defined question. Identifying free text is not needed for ordinary arithmetic replay.

Data linkage requires checks of identifier uniqueness, one-to-many relations, household membership dates and duplicated transfers. A total income comparison is insufficient if a duplicate inclusion cancels an omitted subtraction. Source-component reconciliation should precede the final category comparison. The operator must preserve the exact extraction query and source snapshot hashes in protected run records; reproducibility cannot depend on a live database returning the same values later.

\subsection{Four separate evaluation questions}
\textbf{Rule conformance} compares the authorized implementation with an independently specified reference on complete comparable cases. Report score differences and category differences separately. Let R be the number of category disagreements divided by the number of comparable determinate cases. If that denominator is zero, R is undefined. A zero value demonstrates agreement on that comparison set; it does not establish that the policy measure correctly identifies need.

\textbf{Input-quality robustness} asks how outputs change under prespecified corrections or uncertainty sets for missing, stale and conflicting sources. Each scenario must state which fields change, their admissible values, which cases enter and whether dependencies constrain combinations. Report the changed-category numerator, the paired determinate denominator and excluded/unresolved counts. A transition table is more informative than a single flip rate because movement direction and exception paths matter. A threshold crossing caused by an accurate correction may be intended; a nonzero sensitivity rate alone is not a defect.

\textbf{Welfare validity} requires an independently justified welfare comparator and a sampling design representing the intended population. Old automated decisions and appeal outcomes are not automatically true labels. Appeals are selected by access, knowledge and capacity to contest; non-appellants can also have errors. If a later study estimates inclusion or exclusion errors, it must state the target definition, sampling frame, weights, measurement period, sample size and uncertainty. No such study has been run here.

\textbf{Delivery outcomes} concern notification, resolution time, correction burden, take-up and actual benefit receipt. They should not be inferred from category accuracy alone. Reporting time or cost changes requires actual baseline and follow-up observations with comparable definitions. This manuscript makes no measured claim about savings, processing time or the causal effect of verification on poverty.

OECD/JRC recommends examining how composite outputs depend on variable selection, normalization, imputation, weights and aggregation. Its handbook concerns country-comparison indicators; application to household categories here is a methodological adaptation. Our prespecified scenarios and outer bounds are not a completed variance-based analysis or evidence that any particular weight is justified. \cite{ref23}.

\subsection{Bounded uncertainty without invented imputations}
For an approved additive score only, $S=\sum_j w_jz_j$ with justified bounds a\textsubscript{j} \ensuremath{\leq} z\textsubscript{j} \ensuremath{\leq} b\textsubscript{j} implies $L=\sum_j\min(w_ja_j,w_jb_j)$ and $U=\sum_j\max(w_ja_j,w_jb_j)$. If variables can independently take every value in their declared intervals, these bounds are attainable. If inputs are discrete or linked, the interval is a conservative outer bound and may include unreachable categories. A dependency-aware calculation is then required before asserting that every category in the interval is possible.

The existing synthetic demonstration implements this limited additive case with explicitly artificial variables and categories. It contains seven cases, five complete and two unresolved; four are comparable to supplied reference categories and none of those four disagree. These are software fixtures with stipulated answers. Their successful replay is not a measured Uzbek model accuracy. The demonstration cannot execute a nonlinear decision tree or the complete registry process merely by calling it a score.

The legal audit’s exact fractions are also not a general solution to uncertainty. They settle arithmetic for known inputs. They do not resolve whether an administrative record is current, which legal branch has priority or whether a proxy measures hardship. Each unresolved source or normative choice must be retained rather than converted into an apparently precise number.

\subsection{Release and remediation}
Internal results should first identify whether a disagreement arises from the source, temporal conversion, legal specification, rounding, code or a deliberate policy change. Correction follows the responsible process. Software defects require a corrected version and repeated affected tests; substantive rule changes require competent legal approval. Before/after output comparisons should retain both versions so a later reviewer can reconstruct the cause.

The internal protocol records scope, version, executed cases, comparable and unresolved counts, defects and remedies. Public disclosure follows existing lawful duties or authorization, rather than a new report before every release. Any extract undergoes disclosure and linkage review; small aggregates may identify a person. No arbitrary universal cell-size threshold is treated as statutory anonymity.

The release decision should not turn statistical findings into automatic benefit cuts. An adverse individual consequence requires its own lawful decision and correction/review opportunity. Experimental or shadow calculations must remain distinct from consequential decisions unless a competent legal instrument authorizes their use. No live household category has been changed by this project.

\subsection{Normative necessity: a test does not supply an eligibility condition}
PF-193 paragraphs 13–14 expressly mandate a normative draft for the new multidimensional system. Necessity concerns lawful 2027 assessment content, not every author-supplied technical detail. Material 1 therefore supplies five compact alternative paragraphs for the principal act, replacing rather than supplementing the full seven provisions and annex. They establish the legal basis, implementation conformity, individual decision grounds, competent change and existing implementation process. Internal passports, cases and logs retain their technical role. \cite{ref1}.

Under O‘RQ-682 Articles 3, 14, 17, 18 and 37–40, a legal condition requires the competent normative instrument with applicable form and publication. Departmental normative acts require registration where prescribed. Rules No. 3686 paragraphs 94–95 cover effects on rights and safeguards, interagency obligations and binding effects outside the adopting system; paragraph 96(b) excludes implementation without new norms and 96(v)–(j) provides technical exceptions. A technical title therefore does not remove normative content, while an existing technical document without new norms need not become an act. Paragraph 20 requires necessity and alternatives assessment. This supports narrow incorporation rather than new regulation for its own sake. \cite{ref7}; \cite{ref31}.

Legal and technical coverage are assessed independently. The first identifies the exact source, competence, rank, scope, date and binding consequence; the second identifies the same inputs, dates, exceptions, independently justified answer and version result. Full technical coverage removes duplicate tests but does not establish a missing legal source for a criterion. Equivalent legal coverage removes the additional norm for that issue. If two legally possible choices yield different categories, testing alone cannot select the lawful option. If an existing norm unambiguously selects the answer, implementation and, where necessary, competent clarification are needed, not an artificial amendment.

An authoritative data domain may exclude a particular algebraic input; the conclusion must then narrow. Software validation does not itself prove a legal restriction on reporting a fact. Article 55 does not permit amendment through interpretation. Resolution No. 35 annex 1 paragraph 48 expressly permits temporary algorithm changes during faults, interruptions or incomplete electronic data; this power remains and is not treated as requiring a new act for each temporary change. The proposal records its basis and scope rather than enlarging it. The existence of that mechanism does not establish determination of the entire permanent 2027 methodology. \cite{ref2}; \cite{ref7}.

Shorter normative wording, reference to an applicable rule or a lawfully authorized departmental form constitute substantive adoption where the binding result, competence, scope and dates remain. Technical incorporation of cases alone does not resolve demonstrated unregulated legal content. Integration uses existing processes; actual hours, contract coverage and funds remain unobtained. Resource objections may change technical scope but do not legalize an absent criterion. Universal necessity of all five paragraphs, absence of equivalent current regulation and unique optimality of the author's form have not been established. These are testable limits of the argument, not a promise of unavoidable adoption.

\subsection{Existing controls, proportionality and lawful release management}
Resolution No. 35 already assigns system and source responsibility, protected processing, limited outage powers and oversight. Resolution No. 516 operative paragraph 6$^{1}$ requires positive technical/security conclusions before implementation; annex 1 paragraph 13 provides joint connection tests and paragraph 44 gives access to request statistics. Resolution No. 93 introduces an IT-audit framework and adds the Ministry of Digital Technologies' audit function. These are existing controls to reuse. A successful exchange test does not by itself prove the legal answer to an income/category boundary, and the legal duty to audit does not prove that an Agency-specific scoring audit was completed. No such release inventory has been obtained. \cite{ref2}; \cite{ref26}; \cite{ref27}.

The incremental contribution is the completed derivations and control cases linked to an independently justified answer, date and release result. Equivalent executed checks are reused. This removes duplicate technical work but does not itself establish normative coverage: the legal source is checked separately. Where both coverages are demonstrated, no additional norm is needed; where a legal condition is demonstrably absent, competent normative determination is needed in the principal act or a lawfully available instrument. General duties and constructed cases do not establish the marginal benefit of every clause.

The revised paragraphs 7 and 23 remove a separate compulsory public reporting programme. The approved description is linked on the existing official resource; verification evidence stays in existing release and oversight records. Existing lawful publication, applicant access and authorized disclosure continue. Disclosure review applies to any permitted extract, including linkage between small aggregates. Independent controls run before deployment or relevant change, rather than as a second manual calculation for every application. This avoids adding an uncosted national processing service, but does not claim zero incremental effort.

The Presidential reserve has been removed from the submitted normative draft. Retention of the 2026 method after January, an inclusive income boundary and a transition-debt exemption would introduce distinct entitlement and fiscal choices; no evidence establishing their necessity or affordability was obtained. Optional equality and land-floor research alternatives remain separately labelled in Material 2 and are not conditions of adoption. Paragraph 26 now permits repair and retesting, evidence-based separation of unaffected paths, or use of another conforming software build only under the currently lawful rule. It grants no manual-entry power, expired-method continuation or debt waiver. A missing legal rule goes to the competent developer/adopter before first use; Article 55 interpretation cannot insert amendments. Removing the proposed reserve is not evidence that the official transition is already complete. \cite{ref1}; \cite{ref2}; \cite{ref7}.

\subsection{Responses to substantive objections}
The official 2027 scoring specification has not been obtained for this project. Welfare and fiscal evaluation of that model requires it; the present audit concerns the identified published rules and supports the proposed verification duty. An objection that the land inputs never arise operationally would defeat an incidence claim, which this paper does not make; authoritative geometry and domain evidence should then be incorporated into the passport and tests. An objection that hardship-specific priority already governs would resolve that interpretation issue if its precise legal and operational basis is recorded. It would not justify replacing the special path with a generic interval.

An objection concerning confidentiality is addressed through lawful internal execution and aggregate disclosure review, not by asking for personal records. An objection concerning cost requires a real resource account and may justify staged empirical research; it is not answered by asserting zero cost. If an existing provision already imposes an equivalent duty, the developer should map it to the proposed clause and avoid duplicating it. These responses narrow the disputed propositions; they do not make rejection impossible or oblige the government to adopt the preferred policy.

The December submission date is a deadline in the government mandate, not evidence that this citizen proposal will be included. Outcome tracking should distinguish receipt, a drafting meeting, appointment, acceptance of a clause, portal publication and final adoption. Eventual success requires matching the published act to the proposed clauses. No submission or governmental acceptance has occurred in this workspace.

\section{Established findings and matters requiring finalisation}
The audit covers selected published formulas and predicates, not the complete welfare system. No household microdata, operational software, local tax coefficient records, definitive production rounding or 2027 model weights were available. The land domain is algebraic and constructed. The remittance results depend on explicitly unresolved period interpretations. Special-rule legal interpretation may resolve hardship wording without a substantive policy amendment. Consolidated webpages can change after the working date and must be rechecked before submission.

These limits do not erase the completed findings. The official example is reproduced exactly. The selected recognition/category predicates disagree at P under the declared new-applicant conditions. The hardship interval cannot be represented by either simple general-interval reading without losing a part of the special route. The literal land formula permits negative values within the declared domain, and the remittance contribution depends materially on its unspecified comparison period. Tax and livestock calculations supply additional reproducible benchmarks. None of these results proves the number of people harmed or benefited.

The normative contribution is to make legally consequential computation specifiable, reproducible and reviewable: identify units and periods; preserve exceptions and versions; verify boundaries before deployment; retain unresolved cases; explain decisions; and record independent control calculations within drafting. This is a concrete contribution to formation of the mandated act. Its empirical welfare performance and adoption remain matters for authorized data analysis and competent government action.

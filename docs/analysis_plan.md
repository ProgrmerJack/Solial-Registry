## Objective and legal route

Seek incorporation of enforceable scoring-verification provisions into the normative act mandated by PF-193, rather than introduce another household registry. Adoption and appointment depend on the competent authorities; neither is guaranteed.

[PF-193 of 11 September 2026, paragraphs 13–14](https://lex.uz/docs/-8472526) introduces multidimensional scoring from 1 January 2027 and requires the National Agency for Social Protection and Ministry of Economy and Finance to submit an implementing draft to the Presidential Administration by 1 December 2026. Paragraph 14 does not specify the act's final legal form. The primary Russian submission supplies an insertion module, with aligned Uzbek and English companions. The official state-language draft remains governed by Article 21. The developer must determine the adopting authority and final numbering.

[O‘RQ-682 of 20 April 2021, Articles 20–24 and its drafting-methodology annex](https://lex.uz/docs/-5378966) supplies the proposal, drafting and consultation procedures. The route is a citizen proposal under Article 20; Article 23 requires the developer to study and use citizens’ proposals and scientific recommendations. No working-group inclusion is requested and no government endorsement is claimed.

## Pre-specified scientific question and inputs

Question: does a specified implementation reproduce its approved rules, and which classifications can change under documented input uncertainty or specified parameter changes?

The first stage tested the method on synthetic cases only. The approved next stage audits published legal calculations and their boundary conditions. Neither stage measures poverty-targeting accuracy, exclusion error, discrimination or the effect of social assistance. Those require appropriate reference data and a separate approved empirical protocol.

| Internal decision records | Anonymous research key; application and decision timestamps; source timestamps; legally permitted transformed inputs and quality states; score; category; rule version; correction and review outcomes | Agency and working group must fix exact start/end dates before extraction. Do not request identifiable household microdata for this workspace. ## Method, failure conditions and verification

Use deterministic replay of the approved rules and prespecified sensitivity scenarios. This directly tests rule implementation. Fitting new weights to historical decisions would reproduce those decisions without establishing that they measure need correctly.

Record score and category disagreements separately. Exact equality is required for the reference calculation after applying approved rounding; do not choose a tolerance after inspecting disagreement. For each sensitivity scenario, fix its population, input change, permissible range and denominator before running it. Include combined-input scenarios where relevant. Do not use an arbitrary percentage change as a statutory acceptance threshold.

For an additive rule only, exact interval arithmetic can determine the category range when unresolved inputs may independently take any rational value within their declared closed intervals. For discrete or dependent inputs, this is a conservative outer range and may include unreachable categories. The Python reference also implements the limited legal audit specified below, using rational arithmetic. It does not implement the complete registry decision process, source pipelines, parameter calibration, review workflows or the actual 2027 Social Registry model.

Approach is wrong or incomplete if the rule cannot be formally specified; the reference disagrees with an independent hand calculation; a boundary is ambiguous; an authorised exception is missing; assumed input ranges are invalid; or empirical accuracy is claimed without reference data. A nonzero sensitivity result alone does not establish a defect: some threshold crossings are intended policy distinctions.

Verification: executable tests of threshold equality, fractional inputs, negative coefficients, missing versus zero, duplicate keys, invalid ranges and malformed values; independent hand derivation; reproducible synthetic run; then a fresh-context reviewer. Administrative validation remains unperformed until official rules and lawful data are supplied. Full-cohort descriptive counts do not need invented confidence intervals; sampled accuracy estimates must report sample size, design and uncertainty under the later protocol.

Methodological source: the [OECD/JRC handbook (2008), uncertainty and sensitivity chapter](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/jrc47008_handbook_final.pdf), and the [JRC sensitivity guide, updated 1 December 2020](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/composite-indicators/toolkit_en/navigation-page/10-step-guide_en/step-8-sensitivity-analysis_en), describe examining sensitivity to weights, missing data and aggregation choices. They remain available from the official JRC site. Applying that reasoning to household category boundaries is our proposed adaptation; the sources neither validate Uzbek categories nor supply household weights or acceptance limits.

## Approved analysis: published-rule audit, 30 September 2026

The user explicitly approved starting analysis after the planning stage. Exploratory reading of the current rules identified the candidate boundary and unit issues; these candidates were known before this executable audit was specified. This is a formal audit of published rules, not an empirical household study or a confirmatory population test. The following cases and outcomes are fixed before running the implementation.

**Sources and inputs.** Resolution 35, Annex 1 paragraphs 18–30, 32–33 and 39; PF-258 paragraph 3; [PF-115 of 23 June 2026](https://lex.uz/docs/-8283656), establishing monthly minimum remuneration `M = 1,360,000` from 1 September 2026; and the [National Statistics Committee announcement of 4 March 2026](https://stat.uz/uz/matbuot-markazi/qo-mita-yangiliklar/67179-minimal-istemol-kharazhatlari-ijmati-t-risida-2), giving 2026 monthly minimum consumption expenditure per person `P = 715,000`. September's remuneration rate is used only for a dated single-month calculation, not substituted into earlier income months. Published examples are illustrative legal inputs, not observed household records.

**Method and cases.** Exact deterministic calculation rather than statistical estimation: (1) reproduce the official paragraph 30 example, `6,480,000 / 5 / 3`; (2) evaluate the income predicates at one soum below, exactly at and one soum above `P`, `1.5P` and `2P`, with an able-to-work new applicant, no other refusal grounds and no hardship recommendation; add one former-primary-category control exactly at `P`; (3) apply paragraph 27's literal area formula `UM − QM − min(0.2UM, 200)` to `(UM,QM)` pairs `(0,0), (100,0), (100,80), (100,90), (1000,800), (1000,801), (1001,801), (2000,1801), (2000,0)` in square metres. Income uses the explicitly assumed diagnostic local coefficient `k=1`; its actual local value is not available. The proposed zero floor is computed separately and is not silently applied to the existing rule. (4) calculate all eight paragraph 23 monthly normative-income coefficients and the male/female difference, including its per-person household-income contribution for an explicitly constructed four-member household. No household prevalence is inferred.

**Outcomes and falsifiers.** Record the official-example discrepancy; each literal boundary predicate; negative derived area and income; and exact coefficient contributions. A defect in our transcription, an applicable exception, a higher-ranking rule or a legally restricted domain that removes the counterexample would defeat the corresponding finding. Negative-area cases satisfy only the algebraic domain `0 ≤ QM ≤ UM`; we do not assert that they occur in operational cadastral records. Boundary comparisons omit statutory rounding because no definitive implementation-rounding specification was supplied. They are a request to resolve normative inconsistency, not a declaration of an unlawful individual decision.

**Verification.** Compare with independent integer arithmetic and hand inequalities; test the official published example, equality, the 200-square-metre cap, invalid household sizes and invalid geometry; run the executable audit; obtain one fresh-context review against the official texts. Embed the dated rule extraction, source links and fixture specification in the code and hash that specification in the result. Output is derived and reproducible without fetching mutable webpages; the official text must be rechecked before legal submission.

**Availability check.** Direct retrieval of the current consolidated acts and indexed official-site searches did not supply the 2027 weights. The public consultation portal's observed read-only endpoint `/api/api/aux-jk/v1/open/project` was searched with `statuses=IN_DISCUSSION,DONE`, `size=100`, `page=0`, `orderBy=createdDate`, `sortOrder=desc`. Searches for `Ijtimoiy reyestr`, `koʻp oʻlchovli` and `балли` returned no projects; broader `ijtimoiy` returned 22 projects, none supplying these weights. This is a bounded search result, not proof of nonpublication. Rechecked on 1 October 2026: none of the 40 drafts published for discussion between 11 September and 1 October 2026 is the PF-193 paragraph 14 act. No Agency decision records were available or analysed.

**Drafting contribution.** The proposal supplies normative wording and reproducible control calculations. Internal arithmetic review checks the reference implementation separately from its author.

**Post-audit review check, specified before its implementation.** The fresh-context review demonstrated that the draft's initial blanket change of the borderline lower bound to `>P` conflicts with the hardship route in Resolution 35 paragraphs 34–35. Add one explicitly post-hoc counterexample: a constructed eligible three-member single-parent household, three-month income `8,580,000`, valid recommendation, available quota and no other refusal grounds. Compare ordinary monthly per-person income with the statutory `3/4` adjusted value; test the abandoned `>P` lower bound against the unchanged hardship upper bound `≤1.5P`. Keep this check separate from the original prespecified cases. The ordinary-income and hardship-income branches must be defined separately in the revised amendment. Review also identified PF-258 Annex 1 note (a) as an additional `<P` provision requiring consequential amendment. Neither finding proves any observed household outcome.

## Detailed paper and extension of the approved audit

On 30 September the user instructed us to proceed with deep research, analysis and a detailed paper. This extends the already approved deterministic rule-replay method; no new household estimator, causal model or calibration method is introduced. The question remains whether written rules have reproducible, coherent outputs over their declared domains. Inputs are the current official provisions and explicitly constructed cases, using the dated `P` and `M` above; no Agency observations are introduced. Before the extended execution, fix: all integer building areas `QM=0,…,UM` for `UM=100,1000,1001,2000`; ordinary-income values at `P`, `4P/3`, `1.5P` and `2P`, each with offsets `−1,0,+1`; remittance values `2M−1,2M,2M+1` and unknown, for both country groups, under explicitly separate monthly and three-month-total comparison interpretations; paragraph 24 tax-floor checks at its algebraic crossover with offsets `−1,0,+1` for all eight paragraph 23 coefficient cells; paragraph 25 informal-income contributions for those cells; and goat counts `0,1,14,15,20,100` under paragraph 28. A four-member household divisor is illustrative, not a sampled cohort.

Outcomes are exact contribution values, jumps, admissibility predicates and domain failures. Remittance models remain conditional interpretations because the official comparison period was not supplied; do not select whichever interpretation produces a preferred result. The hardship audit compares both ordinary- and adjusted-income readings of the general category interval with the separate paragraphs 34–35 path. A special-rule priority may resolve interpretation legally; the analysis asks for that priority to be explicit in the executable specification. Failure conditions remain a transcription error, a missing exception, inconsistent units or a domain restriction that removes a counterexample. Verify algebraic boundaries independently, run regression tests, and commission one fresh-context review of the extended paper and code. Grid counts describe generated test cases, not the frequency of affected households. No confidence intervals or estimated policy effects will be manufactured.

## Reproduction and workspace structure

`data/rules/legal_specification.json` stores the normalized dated legal extraction and frozen cases; `data/fixtures/additive_demo.json` stores the artificial additive example. These are author-extracted inputs, not raw household data or full archival copies of the legislation. `src/social_registry/` separates exact numeric validation, additive replay, published-rule calculations and provenance. `tests/` holds boundary and provenance regressions. `scripts/` holds the audit CLI, independent algebra verification, CSV export, monochrome figure rendering, XeLaTeX compilation and the complete pipeline. `results/` separates audit JSON, tables, vector figures, execution logs and documents. Earlier presentation outputs are preserved under `results/archive/` and are not the submission documents.

Run the complete project:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_project.py
```

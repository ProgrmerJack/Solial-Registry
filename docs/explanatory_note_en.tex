The structure follows paragraph 84 of the Unified Methodology appended to the Law “On Normative Legal Acts”; the scientific and legal justification is set out in Section~\ref{sec:sci} as other information needed to justify the draft.

\section{Basis for preparation and necessity}\label{sec:basis}
Subparagraph “a” of paragraph 13 of Decree PF-193 provides that from 1 January 2027 need is studied and families are included in the Social Registry through a multidimensional assessment system that takes into account, \textit{in addition to the family’s financial condition}, chronic illness, disability, external labour migration and large family size. Subparagraph “b” provides for a point-based scoring of socio-economic condition and assignment of families to categories by its results. Paragraph 14 instructs the National Agency for Social Protection under the President of the Republic of Uzbekistan (hereinafter, the Agency), jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, to submit the draft normative legal act to the Presidential Administration by 1 December 2026\cite{pf193}.

The need for the proposed norms is supported by the following.

\textbf{First, the decision is made automatically.} Under stage 6 of the Scheme for Including Families in the Social Registry (Annex 1 to the Regulation on the Procedure for Maintaining the Social Registry), the “Ijtimoiy reyestr” information module recognizes or refuses a family and assigns its category “automatically”\cite{res35}. In a point-based system the category depends on which side of a threshold the score falls. Every threshold, its equality case and every exception therefore directly determine a family’s access to assistance.

\textbf{Second, the law requires an automated decision to be explained.} Article 24 of the Law “On Personal Data” obliges the owner and/or operator to explain to the data subject the procedure for a decision based solely on automated processing and its possible legal consequences, to give the opportunity to object, and to notify the result of considering the objection in writing within ten days\cite{pdlaw}. Points 37 and 44 of the Regulation provide only an SMS about inclusion, refusal, a change of category or removal from the Registry\cite{res35}. If artificial-intelligence technologies are used in the multidimensional assessment, Article 7\textsuperscript{1} of the Law “On Informatization” prohibits relying solely on their conclusions when taking legally significant decisions on human rights, and the Ethics Rules (registration No. 3787) require the logic, main algorithmic criteria and data sources to be open and explainable and the final decision to be taken by a human\cite{inform,ethics}. Points 3, 7, 17 and 18 of Section I of Material 1 require such explainability whatever the calculation method.

\textbf{Third, the decision is an administrative act.} The Law “On Administrative Procedures” applies to registration procedures and procedures related to the provision of public services (Article 3, part one); the Regulation treats the actions for including a family in the Social Registry as public services (point 47), and Annex 4 to Resolution No. 35 calls the acts of the “mahalla seven” on maintaining the Registry administrative acts and requires complaints against them to be considered under the legislation on administrative procedures (points 3 and 11); the Regulation itself refers disputes on inclusion, category, reassessment and removal to the appeal councils (point 50)\cite{res35}. A decision on inclusion, refusal, change of category or removal creates, changes or terminates public-law relations with a specific family and therefore meets the definition of an administrative act (Article 4). Such an act must contain a reasoning part with all factual and legal grounds and the time limit and procedure for appeal; the body may not rely on evidence that participants were not given the opportunity to examine (Articles 53–54). Information submitted by an interested person is considered reliable until the contrary is proved, and information held by other bodies is obtained by the administrative body itself on the one-window principle (Articles 8 and 14); acts establishing special procedures must not worsen the position of persons (Article 2, part two)\cite{apl}. Points 37 and 44 of the Regulation provide only an SMS about the decision itself and no access to these elements.

\textbf{Fourth, practice shows unjustified refusals.} The Government portal reported on 17 July 2026 that in 184 mahallas all 2,000 applications received in one month were refused without grounds; appeals to the President on Registry inclusion rose one and a half times since the start of the year, from 30,000 to 46,000; and the Prosecutor General’s Office was instructed to strictly oversee proper maintenance of the Registry\cite{govuz}. The report does not attribute these refusals to the formulas examined in this note; it shows that wrong decisions can be detected and corrected only if the grounds of each decision are retained and reproducible. The World Bank’s 2026 poverty and equity assessment states that the targeting formula was revised in early 2026 and that an assessment of its effectiveness is not yet available\cite{wb2026}.

\textbf{Fifth, four inconsistencies have been demonstrated in the current calculation rules.} Article 23 of the Law “On Normative Legal Acts” obliges the developer to identify gaps and contradictions that adversely affect legal regulation\cite{orq682}; the inconsistencies below are such. Three of them (points 26, 27 and 33 of the Regulation) are removed by the amendments in Section II of Material 1 (Table 1); the fourth, concerning families in difficult life circumstances, requires the developer’s decision and is set out in Section~\ref{sec:q}. In addition, points 37 and 44 of the Regulation do not secure the requirements of Article 24 of the Law “On Personal Data” and Articles 53–54 of the Law “On Administrative Procedures”. Because the family’s financial condition remains a criterion from 1 January 2027, the income calculation rules continue to determine the assessment result until replaced.

\section{Purposes, tasks and main provisions}
The purpose is to ensure that each family’s assessment result follows only from approved rules, can be reproduced from retained data and is explained to the applicant. The tasks are: remove uncertainty at threshold values; prevent the application of a methodology that conflicts with acts of higher legal force; check software before changes are applied; retain the grounds of each result; and eliminate the four demonstrated inconsistencies in the current Regulation.

\section{Regulation at the level of law}
The proposals relate to the subordinate act mandated by paragraph 14 of Decree PF-193. No draft law is proposed.

\section{Expected results}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.27cm]
\item No household-plot area calculation gives a negative value (at present this occurs where $QM>0.8\,UM$ for plots up to 1,000 m² and where $QM>UM-200$ for plots over 1,000 m²).
\item For each threshold of the methodology the category at equality is determined; a family with income equal to minimum consumption expenditure receives a determinate result.
\item A family’s counted income does not fall when its actual remittance increases.
\item Each assessment result can be reproduced from retained data; all decisions affected by an error are identified within ten working days of its confirmation by control calculation.
\item On inclusion, refusal, change of category and removal, the applicant receives the grounds of the decision, information on its legal consequences, the objection and rights-protection procedure, and the time limit and procedure for appeal, as required by Article 24, part three, of the Law “On Personal Data” and Articles 53–54 of the Law “On Administrative Procedures”.
\item The new methodology and each change to it are not applied until each requirement of point 11 of Section I is covered by control examples and the result at the boundaries of intervals set by acts of higher legal force than the methodology matches those acts; if these conditions are not met by the established date of application, the transition procedure is determined by the body that approved the methodology.
\item A missing reply from an information system does not in itself lead to refusal, change of category or removal of a family; until a reassessment is completed its results are not applied.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Foreign experience}
Chile’s Ministry of Social Development and Family publishes the procedure for calculating the socio-economic rating of the Social Household Registry, approved by Resolution No. 082 of 15 November 2023, with worked calculation examples, thresholds stated as “equal to or greater than” and an explicit precedence rule when several checks are triggered for a household\cite{chile}. The World Bank notes that outdated registry data lead to both inclusion and exclusion errors, and that grievance redress mechanisms enhance transparency and accountability\cite{wb2025}.

\section{Description of content and a question for the developer’s decision}\label{sec:q}
Section I of Material 1 (23 points, each as a rule containing one legal norm in accordance with Methodology paragraph 22) is included in the act being prepared. Section II (5 points) amends the current Regulation and Section III (3 points) contains a transitional provision; if the new act replaces the Regulation, the same rules are included in its norms.

\textbf{Question for the developer’s decision.} For families in difficult life circumstances, subparagraph “v” of paragraph 3 of Decree PF-258, together with item (i) of note (b) to its Annex 1, assigns to the “family at the poverty boundary” category families whose income, calculated with the 25\,\% reduction coefficient, is from $P$ to $1.5P$\cite{pf258}. Points 34–35 of the Regulation set for the same procedure the conditions $D>P$ and $A=0.75D\le1.5P$, that is $P<D\le2P$, with no lower bound on reduced income. The Regulation therefore includes families with $P<D<4P/3$ whose reduced income is below $P$: for example, a family with income of $858{,}000$ soum ($1.2P$; reduced income $643{,}500$ soum) is included under point 35 but does not fit the Decree’s interval. In addition, subpoint “v” of point 39 of the Regulation does not state that the interval applies to reduced income for these families; applied to ordinary income it would exclude families with $1.5P<D\le2P$ (for example, $1{,}287{,}000$ soum). Once subpoint “a” of point 33 is amended (Material 1, Section II, point 3), a family with $D=P$ also enters this procedure, so the mismatch covers $P\le D<4P/3$; the condition $A\ge P$ also removes this case. One of two options must be chosen: add the condition $A\ge P$ to points 34–35 of the Regulation, which excludes families with $P<D<4P/3$ from new inclusion, and the adopted norm then includes a transitional provision like Section III of Material 1: families already included retain the category until the end of the twelve-month period under the fourth paragraph of point 42 of the Regulation; or amend Decree PF-258 so that the interval for this procedure matches points 34–35. Under either option, subpoint “v” of point 39 states that reduced income applies to these families. If an interval of the new methodology is established by an act of higher legal force than the methodology itself, such an inconsistency is detected before application: the norm of the act establishing an interval is stated (Section I, point 7(b)), control examples with the result under that act itself are made at its boundaries (point 11(e), point 12), and application is not permitted while a difference remains, unless the body that approved the methodology has determined the transition procedure for the established date (point 14).

\section{Financial and economic justification}\label{sec:fin}
The need for expenditure to implement the act, its amount and sources of coverage are determined by the developer of the draft (Article 23 of the Law “On Normative Legal Acts”\cite{orq682}); their components are set out below. The proposed funding approach is implementation within the funds provided for maintaining and developing the YAMIH system (Material 1, Section I, point 23), subject to the Agency’s assessment of its capacity and the fiscal consequences:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.27cm]
\item No new bodies, posts, information systems or application stages are created; the existing YAMIH system, commissioning procedure, personal account on the Unified Portal of Interactive Public Services and Inson centres are used.
\item The set of control examples and the program calculating them have been prepared by the author and are provided free of charge with these materials.
\item Agency resources will be needed for changes to the software and applicant explanations, retention of the information needed to repeat calculations, control calculations and review of affected decisions. The scope of this work depends on the design of the YAMIH system and existing contracts and is determined by the Agency from its data; reuse of existing control examples (point 16) avoids duplicate work.
\item Correcting wrongful refusals revealed by verification may increase assistance expenditure. This is a lawful consequence of applying the current rules correctly, not a new benefit. The family categories and conditions of assistance are established by Decree PF-258, and its paragraph 14 instructs the Ministry of Economy and Finance to provide for the expenses of implementing the Decree in the State budget parameters from 2026\cite{pf258}; assistance to families entitled to it under that Decree is such an expense.
\item Integration of the YAMIH system with the Tax Committee’s information systems to obtain the land-tax coefficients approved for each mahalla, which are used to calculate normative income from a household plot, is provided for by measure 2 of item 6 of the roadmap (Annex 3 to Decree PF-258; implementers: the Tax Committee and the Agency)\cite{pf258}. The amendment of point 27 adds only a zero floor to the formula and requires no new integration.
\item The amendments to points 26, 27 and 33 do not enlarge the circle of recipients: counted income does not fall, and a case without a category is removed. At the same time, the amendments to points 26, 27 and 33 may make some families ineligible. The amendment to point 27 raises a family’s counted income by at most $95{,}200k$ soum per month, where $k$ is the mahalla coefficient, and may take a family above the threshold. The amendment to point 26 raises to $3M$ the counted income of families with transfers from Annex 6 countries between $2M$ and $3M$ per month: for example, a family of four whose only income is a transfer of $2{,}720{,}000$ soum per month is now counted at $680{,}000$ soum per person (below $P$) and after the amendment at $1{,}020{,}000$ soum (above $P$). The amendment to point 33 leads to refusal of inclusion for families with income exactly equal to $P$ who are now recognized under point 32 without a category.
\item Removing the non-monotonicity does not in itself require choosing the higher normative income. For other countries the current norm already compares the transfer with a threshold equal to the normative income ($2M$); the main option extends the same principle to the Annex 6 countries. The alternative — a normative income of $2M$ for Annex 6 countries — enlarges the circle of recipients and requires expenditure. It is proposed that the option be chosen after the Agency assesses the aggregate impact of both options on its data. Section III of Material 1 protects families already included against a change of category or removal caused solely by the amendments: for a “state-supported family” and a “poor family” until the questionnaire is re-completed (second paragraph of point 40 of the Regulation: every 12 and 6 months), and for a “family at the poverty boundary” until the end of the twelve-month period under the fourth paragraph of point 42 of the Regulation.
\end{enumerate}
The exact number of families affected by each amendment is determined by the Agency on its data using the attached program, without transferring personal data outside the Agency.

\section{Discussion and coordination}
The proposal is submitted under Article 20 of the Law “On Normative Legal Acts” and Article 3 of the Law “On Appeals of Individuals and Legal Entities”. Discussion on the portal and coordination with interested bodies are conducted by the developer. \section{Scientific and legal justification}\label{sec:sci}
This Section contains other information needed to justify the draft (Methodology paragraph 84): the research question, input data, method, results, their link to the proposed norms and the procedure for reproducing the calculations.

\subsection{Research question and input data}
The research question is stated so that it can be refuted: \textbf{do the published income and category rules give a single, internally consistent result at the thresholds and throughout the declared input domain?} The finding is refuted by an error in transcribing a rule, an omitted applicable exception, a narrower lawful input domain or a disagreement with independent arithmetic.

Official texts of the acts on lex.uz as of 30 September — 1 October 2026 were used: Decree PF-193, Resolution No. 35 (consolidated with the amendments made by Cabinet Resolutions No. 271 of 21 May 2026 and No. 393 of 20 July 2026), Decree PF-258, and the Laws “On Normative Legal Acts”, “On Personal Data”, “On Appeals of Individuals and Legal Entities”, “On Administrative Procedures” and “On Informatization”\cite{pf193,res35,pf258,orq682,pdlaw,appeals,apl,inform}. Resolution No. 35 and Decree PF-193 are officially published in the state language; amendment wording follows the Uzbek text.

Monthly minimum consumption expenditure per person in 2026 is $P=715{,}000$ soum\cite{mpr}; the minimum wage from 1 September 2026 is $M=1{,}360{,}000$ soum\cite{mrot}.

\subsection{Method}
\subsubsection{Objects and units}
Let $Y$ be the family’s counted income over three months and $N$ the counted number of members; monthly income per person is $D=Y/(3N)$ (point 18 of the Regulation). The formulas for individual income contributions and the category conditions are checked; full aggregation of $Y$ from administrative data is not reproduced. The September 2026 value of $M$ is used only in dated single-month calculations.

Calculations are performed by the reference program (electronic supplement). Numbers are entered as integers or strings of decimal and rational numbers; exact rational arithmetic is used, so equality comparisons are not distorted by binary rounding. Floating-point numbers, invalid divisors and incomplete data are rejected.

\subsubsection{Prespecified plan and case selection}
The list of checks and the case grids were fixed in the analysis plan before calculations were run. Income grid: $P$, $1.5P$, $2P$ with offsets of $-1$, $0$, $+1$ soum. Difficult life circumstances: values around $P$, $4P/3$, $1.5P$, $2P$. Land formula: all integer building areas $QM$ from $0$ to $UM$ for $UM=100$, $1{,}000$, $1{,}001$, $2{,}000$ m² (4,105 cases in total). Remittances: an unknown value and values around $2M$ for both country groups under two possible comparison periods. Tax base, self-employment and goats: prespecified points.

The grids are constructed domains, not a sample of families. The number of violating cases in a grid characterises the formula’s domain, not the share of families in Uzbekistan; shares of families can be determined only on the Agency’s data (Section~\ref{sec:agency}).

\subsubsection{Legal computational conditions}
Subpoint “a” of point 33 refuses recognition as a “state-supported family” or “poor family” where $D>P$; point 32 recognizes the family where there is no refusal ground; subpoints “a” and “b” of point 39 and point 3 of Decree PF-258 assign families with $D<P$ to these categories\cite{res35,pf258}.

For difficult life circumstances $A=3D/4$. Points 34–35 of the Regulation allow inclusion in the “family at the poverty boundary” category where $D>P$ and $A\le1.5P$. Subparagraph “v” of paragraph 3 of Decree PF-258, with item (i) of note (b) to Annex 1, sets for this procedure the interval from $P$ to $1.5P$ on reduced income $A$; subpoint “v” of point 39 of the Regulation repeats the interval without naming the type of income.

Household plot area (point 27): $TEM=UM-QM-\min(0.2\,UM;\,200)$ m² over the domain $0\le QM\le UM$; monthly normative income $TEM\cdot0.035\,M\cdot k/100$, where 100 m² is one sotix and $k$ the mahalla land-tax coefficient. The calculations take $k=1$ because actual mahalla coefficients were not used; for another $k$ the amounts are multiplied by $k$.

Remittances (point 26): if the amount of transfers is not determined or is less than $2M$, normative income $cM$ applies, where $c=3$ for countries listed in Annex 6 and $c=2$ for other countries. Because the comparison period is not stated, both readings were calculated: comparison of a monthly amount $q$ and of a three-month total.

\subsubsection{Verification of results}
Results were verified in four ways: reproduction of the official example in point 30; manual inequalities; a separate checking program that does not use the calculation module and recomputes all 4,105 land cases, 12 hardship cases, 24 tax cases, 16 remittance cases and 6 goat cases with integer expressions; and automated tests. All 39 tests pass (17 checks of the Regulation’s rules, 12 checks of the synthetic scoring example and 10 provenance checks that reject an outdated, altered or incomplete result).

\subsection{Results}
\subsubsection{Official example and equality to the threshold}\label{res:eq}
The example in point 30 of the Regulation gives $6{,}480{,}000/(5\cdot3)=\mathbf{432{,}000}$ soum per person per month; the difference from the published answer is zero.

At $D=P=715{,}000$ both conditions $D>P$ and $D<P$ are false. The refusal ground of subpoint “a” of point 33 does not apply and, absent other refusal grounds, the family is recognized under point 32, but it does not satisfy the condition of categories “a” and “b” of point 39. A first-time applicant without a “mahalla seven” recommendation is assigned to no category. The control case of a family previously removed from a primary category falls, at $D=P$, within the interval of subpoint “v” of point 39, confirming that the cases are correctly separated.

The proposed amendment of subpoint “a” of point 33 (“equal to or higher” instead of “higher”) closes this case without amending Decree PF-258: the refusal condition becomes the exact complement of the category condition $D<P$ in point 3 of the Decree.

\subsubsection{Difficult life circumstances: the Regulation is wider than the Decree}\label{res:hard}
The conditions of the special procedure simplify exactly:
\begin{equation}
D>P,\quad\frac34D\le\frac32P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P<D\le2P.
\end{equation}
Under the Decree the interval applies to reduced income: the condition $A\ge P$ is equivalent to $D\ge4P/3$. The Regulation additionally includes families with $P<D<4P/3$; example: $D=858{,}000$ soum ($1.2P$), reduced income $643{,}500<P$. Subpoint “v” of point 39 of the Regulation does not name the type of income; applied to ordinary income it would exclude families with $1.5P<D\le2P$; example: $D=1{,}287{,}000$ soum ($1.8P$) (Figure 1). At $P=715{,}000$: $4P/3=2{,}860{,}000/3$, $1.5P=1{,}072{,}500$, $2P=1{,}430{,}000$ soum.

The errors were found precisely at thresholds, in exceptions and in units. Random control examples may miss them: for instance, in an income grid with a one-soum step the equality $D=P$ is one case of three around the threshold, and in a real income distribution it is a rare value. That is why point 11 of Section I of Material 1 requires the example set to include each threshold, the values next to it and each exception. Passing a finite set of examples does not prove the absence of every error, but the protocol (point 15(d)) shows the coverage of each requirement of point 11, and a gap blocks application; on the established date of application the transition procedure in that case is determined by the body that approved the methodology (point 14).

\subsection{Application to the Agency’s data}\label{sec:agency}
The inconsistencies arise for the specified admissible inputs regardless of their frequency; the calculations do not establish how the current software handles those inputs or how many families are affected. The Agency can determine that number on its data with the attached program without transferring personal data outside the Agency:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.27cm]
\item plots with $QM>0.8\,UM$ (where $UM\le1{,}000$ m²) or $QM>UM-200$ (where $UM>1{,}000$ m²) — affected by the correction of point 27;
\item families with $D=P$ — affected by the correction of subpoint “a” of point 33;
\item families whose counted income differs between the current and the proposed rule: under the monthly reading, transfers from Annex 6 countries between $2M$ and $3M$ per month; under the three-month-total reading, also transfers from any country with a monthly average between $2M/3$ and $2M$ — affected by the correction of point 26;
\item families included under point 35 with $P<D<4P/3$ (reduced income below $P$) — affected by the question in Subsection~\ref{res:hard}.
\end{enumerate}
For each group the program compares the result of the current and the proposed rule. The calculation is run inside the Agency by an authorized operator; only aggregate numbers leave the Agency.

\subsection{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.27cm]
\item The published calculation rules are reproducible: the official example gives 432,000 soum with no difference.
\item Inconsistencies are demonstrated in four rules: an undetermined result at income equal to $P$; inclusion by the Regulation of hardship families with reduced income below $P$, not provided for by Decree PF-258; a negative area where buildings cover more than 80\,\% of a plot of up to 1,000 m² (for larger plots, where $QM>UM-200$); and a fall in counted income as a transfer rises.
\item Each inconsistency is removed by amending the text of the norm (Material 1, Section II) or by the developer’s decision (Section~\ref{sec:q}), not by a software setting.
\item For the new point-based methodology, Section I of Material 1 prohibits the same classes of error (undetermined category at equality — point 4; several results where exceptions apply — point 5; negative values and decreasing counted income — point 3(g); an interval conflicting with an act of higher legal force — points 7(b), 11(e), 12) and does not permit the methodology to be applied until each such place has been checked by a control example without differences (point 14); the grounds of each decision are retained (point 17).
\end{enumerate}
